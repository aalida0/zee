This paper **already makes and numerically studies precisely the qualitative link** originally proposed as Candidate A: a larger Zee cubic scalar coupling produces larger doublet–singlet charged mixing but can generate deeper charge-breaking minima; the authors report upper mixing-angle regions as functions of the charged scalar masses and selected quartics in Fig. 5. This is not an incidental reference; it directly challenges novelty of the headline *"vacuum stability limits charged mixing"*.

Model limits: the 2020 study works in the CP-conserving limit, using a Higgs-basis scalar potential more general in some terms than the frozen softly-Z2-broken Type-II scalar implementation, but simplifying several quartic couplings and targeting a leptophilic model. Its vacuum evaluation is a **numerical stationary-minimum search** with selected parameter choices, not a demonstrated exact all-field global certificate. The 2020 numerical mixing envelope cannot be transplanted uncritically to our exact scalar input cards.

## Consequence for current evidence

The committed post-run P2 result `mu4 <= ~1316.133373 GeV` is a **fixed-field necessary condition** for a specified, fixed-coefficient tree potential slice, with an exact field-value counterexample immediately above that threshold. It does not prove P2 globality below the threshold, give an all-Zee necessary limit, or show a globally optimized threshold. The relative mixing/cascade behavior from that slice is therefore a bounded numerical illustration, **not a new qualitative vacuum–mixing correlation**. The original report correctly does not claim it is a novel paper result.

A truly differentiated scalar-theory paper would require an independently source-checked, **reusable**, exact/interval-verified global-vacuum theorem or substantially improved quantitative bound **beyond the 2005, 2020 and 2021 analyses**, with demonstrated material consequences for a nontrivial domain, not only a selected P2 line. This has **not** been established.
